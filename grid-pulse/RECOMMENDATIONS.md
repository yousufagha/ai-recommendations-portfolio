# Draft recommendations

Generated by `pipeline/render_recommendations.py`. Analysis of public AEMO data, not financial or investment advice. FY means July to June. Figures are FY2026 unless a range is shown, and the range covers FY2023 to FY2026.

## 1. Retailers: hold South Australian cap cover through mild years

**In plain English:** a tiny share of very expensive moments made up 35% of South Australia's wholesale power cost, so insurance against them is worth keeping even after a quiet year.

**Finding:** in FY2026, the dearest 1% of intervals carried 35% of SA wholesale cost, against 8% in Victoria and 6% in Queensland. The gross payoff of a $300 cap was $34.9/MWh of load in SA, $7.6 in NSW and $1.1 in Victoria.

**Recommendation:** set cover from several years of tail risk, not last year's outcome, and treat SA separately. In NSW the same payoff ranged from $7.6 to $48.9/MWh, so one mild year says little.

**Limits:** gross of premium, because contract prices are not in this data. Whether cover is good value depends on the premium paid.

## 2. Large energy users: move flexible load out of the evening peak

**In plain English:** power costs far less in the middle of the day than in the early evening, so a business that can run the same equipment a few hours earlier could save real money.

**Finding:** moving 1 MW for 4 hours each weekday from the 16:00 to 21:00 window to the 10:00 to 16:00 window avoided $151,760 a year at spot prices in SA, $96,946 in Queensland, $79,016 in NSW and $78,811 in Victoria.

**Recommendation:** prioritise shifting in SA and Queensland, and test the case at FY2026 prices rather than the peak year. The range is $151,760 to $260,278 in SA and $78,811 to $204,940 in Victoria.

**Limits:** spot exposure only. Network charges, retail contracts and operating costs are excluded, and a contract customer may capture little of it.

## 3. Storage investors: size cases well below the headline spread

**In plain English:** a battery makes money by buying power when it is cheap and selling when it is dear. These figures are the best case, so a real battery should be expected to earn less from this.

**Finding:** a 1 MW, 2-hour battery cycling once a day had an upper bound of $245,279 a year in SA, $140,149 in NSW and $102,177 in Victoria. SA ranged from $245,279 to $386,943.

**Recommendation:** treat these as ceilings when screening regions. Stress any investment case at the low end of the range, given how far the figures moved in one year.

**Limits:** perfect foresight, 85% round-trip efficiency, no degradation, no price impact, no ancillary services and no ordering constraint. Achievable revenue will differ and may be materially lower on energy alone.

## 4. Policy makers and networks: price daytime surplus into demand

**In plain English:** for 26% of the time in Victoria, the wholesale price of power was below zero, yet many households may never see that. Pricing could encourage people to use power at those times.

**Finding:** prices were negative for 26% of the time in Victoria and 31% in SA, up from 19% and 23% in FY2023. NSW reached 12% and Queensland 17%. The rise is not steady: July to October this year was lower than the same months a year earlier, at 18.9% against 23.8% in Victoria and 23.9% against 31.6% in SA.

**Recommendation:** tariffs and demand programmes should reward daytime consumption in these regions, because many hours have a market signal that retail customers may not see. Review this if the recent fall continues.

**Limits:** this data shows the price signal and not its causes. Attribution to solar needs the fuel-mix data planned for Phase 3.
